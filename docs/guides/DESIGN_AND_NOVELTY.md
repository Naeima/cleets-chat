# CLEETS-CHAT: description and novelty statement

Everything below is drawn from the uploaded code (`app.py`, `kg_service.py`, `qa.py`, `prediction_builder.py`, `README.md`) and the two graphs (`cleets_cskg_enriched.ttl`, 96,479 triples; `cleets_prediction_kg.ttl`, 57,612 triples). Where a claim goes beyond what the code does today, it is marked as such in the final section.

## Short version (abstract or web page, about 120 words)

CLEETS-CHAT is a question-answering interface over the CLEETS knowledge graphs for electric-vehicle adoption in Wales. Users ask free-text questions about observed data (private battery-electric vehicle keepership, public charge points, population, density and income deprivation for the 22 Welsh local authority districts, 2009 to 2026) or about scenario predictions to 2045. Every answer is composed from retrieved RDF statements or from deterministic calculations over the graph; no generative language model is involved, so the system cannot state a fact that is absent from its knowledge base. Each answer carries active links to the official source datasets and to the CLEETS knowledge graph, and predictions are labelled as model outputs with their generating method and scenario. The system runs on rdflib and Dash with no external services.

## What it does

**Knowledge base.** Two Turtle graphs share the CLEETS ontology (OWL 2, with bundled SHACL shapes). The observed graph, CLEETS-KG-Enriched, holds 10,458 observations covering private BEV keepership (DfT VEH0132), private licensed vehicle stock (DfT VEH0105), public charge points (DfT EVCI9001), resident population (StatsWales), population density (ONS), WIMD 2025 income deprivation and three derived indicators (chargers per 100,000 residents, BEVs per 1,000 residents, keepers per charger). Every observation cites its source dataset through `dcterms:source`; each dataset record carries a rendered bibliographic citation, licence, publisher, coverage and landing page; derived indicators point through `prov:wasGeneratedBy` to an activity whose comment states the formula and its verification. The prediction graph holds 5,520 quarterly per-district predictions (2026-Q1 to 2046-Q3) under three scenarios, plus 198 milestone predictions (the first quarter in which a district crosses a given adoption share). Each prediction carries `dcterms:source` (VEH0132, VEH0105) and `prov:wasGeneratedBy` a documented activity: per-district logistic diffusion curves fitted to the quarterly adoption share, recency-weighted and shrunk toward the Wales anchor, with one midpoint shift per scenario that sets the Wales-wide private BEV share at 2045-Q4 to 95%, 99% or 99.9% of the private vehicle stock. The class `cleets:EVAdoptionPrediction` is annotated "Not an observed value".

**Two query modes.** In Actual Data mode the engine tokenises the question, scores every triple of the observed graph by lexical overlap with the subject's local name, its label, the predicate and the object, groups the best matches by subject, and prints them as evidence with the source links resolved from the graph. In Prediction mode a small rule set recognises districts (by label), years (by regex, default 2045) and scenarios (low, central, high), and answers five question families: a district's prediction under a scenario, a three-scenario comparison, highest and lowest rankings, a Wales summary, and a provenance description. A data-inventory request in either mode returns counts of triples, classes, datasets and scenarios.

**Answer composition.** Answers are Markdown assembled from retrieved values and fixed templates. Each ends with a Sources line built from the graph (dataset label linked to its landing page, plus the CLEETS knowledge graph), and prediction answers add the sentence "This is a model prediction, not an observed value." When no statement matches, the system says so and declines to infer.

**Interface.** A Dash application with a mode switch, a free-text box, a help panel of example question families, an inventory button and a footer reporting the mode and the retrieval time. The prediction graph loads at start-up; the observed graph loads on first use.

## What is new

1. **Citation by construction rather than by generation.** The links under an answer are not produced by a model; they are read from the provenance layer of the graph (`dcterms:source`, `prov:wasDerivedFrom`, `prov:wasGeneratedBy`, `dcat:landingPage`). SHACL shapes in the graph require every dataset record to be citable and every observation, population figure and district to cite a dataset, so citability is a validated property of the data, not a feature of the chatbot.

2. **Evidence-bounded answering with no generative model.** Because the answer text is templated over retrieved statements and deterministic computations, the system cannot assert anything that is not in the graph. This is the sense in which "hallucination-free" is exact rather than aspirational, and it is verifiable by reading `qa.py`.

3. **Explicit epistemic status.** Observed and predicted knowledge live in separate graphs, are queried in separate modes, and predictions are typed and labelled as model outputs with their scenario and generating activity attached. A reader of any answer can tell whether a number was measured, calculated or forecast.

4. **A prediction knowledge graph as a first-class, citable resource.** Scenario semantics (terminal target share, midpoint shift), the fitting method and the source datasets are represented in RDF alongside the predicted values, under a CC BY 4.0 dataset record. Forecasts are therefore queryable, traceable and re-usable like observed data, rather than opaque model artefacts.

5. **An ontology-grounded, open-data knowledge base for EV adoption in Wales.** To our knowledge no existing question-answering tool integrates DfT vehicle licensing and charging statistics with StatsWales, ONS and WIMD data at local-authority level under a published ontology with dataset-level citations. (This claim needs a literature check before it appears in print.)

6. **Reproducibility and auditability.** The stack is rdflib and Dash, with no external APIs, model weights or non-deterministic components; the same question yields the same answer and the same sources on any machine.

## Positioning (for a related-work paragraph)

Retrieval-augmented generation chatbots retrieve passages and let a language model write the answer, so fabricated or mis-attributed statements remain possible; text-to-SPARQL systems let a model write the query, so wrong queries yield confident wrong answers; classic template-based knowledge-graph question answering is deterministic but rarely carries dataset-level provenance into the answer. CLEETS-CHAT sits with the third family in mechanism and adds the provenance layer and the observed-versus-predicted separation. State this positioning with references of your choosing; the code supports the mechanism claims, not the survey.

## Claims to qualify before publication (mock review)

- **"AI" in the title.** There is no learned component in the question-answering path. Reviewers will ask what the AI is. Either present the system as evidence-bounded, LLM-free question answering, or add a constrained language model for phrasing and keep the citation and evidence guarantees at the retrieval layer.
- **The help panel promises more than `qa.py` delivers.** "Which LADs have high EV uptake but relatively low charger provision?", "How does income deprivation relate to EV adoption?", "Why is Cardiff's predicted value higher than Swansea's?" and "How sensitive is Cardiff to the scenario assumption?" are listed, but Actual mode returns raw triples ranked by token overlap and the "why" question returns Cardiff's value alone. "Which LAD has the highest EV keepership?" in Actual mode is not computed either. Implement these as deterministic SPARQL or pandas computations, or remove them from the panel before a demo or paper.
- **Retrieval is lexical.** Ranking by counting matched tokens over the first few thousand hits favours provenance triples over value triples and cannot handle synonyms or numeric intent. A SPARQL template layer for the common (district, indicator, period) questions would answer most of the panel exactly.
- **`prediction_builder.py` contradicts the shipped prediction graph.** The builder writes a `pred:` namespace, `cleets:underScenario`, `cleets:predictedEVKeepership` and `cleets:predictionYear`, and attributes results to a "ScenarioAwareGNN" with carrying-capacity multipliers of 6, 10 and 15; the shipped graph uses `cleets:scenario`, `cleets:predictedKeepership`, `cleets:forTime` and a logistic-diffusion activity, and `kg_service.py` reads only the latter. Anyone reading the repository will conclude that the forecasts come from a GNN placeholder. Remove or update the builder.
- **Citations are underused.** The enriched graph carries full Harvard citations, licences and access URLs for every dataset, but the app prints only a label linked to a landing page, and prediction rankings cite the knowledge graph alone rather than the DfT tables. Rendering `dcterms:bibliographicCitation` gives a real reference list at no extra cost.
- **Traceability is not correctness.** The guarantees concern attribution, not truth. The forecasts assume a fixed private vehicle stock and near-complete BEV penetration by 2045 under all three scenarios; the chatbot states that a value is a prediction but surfaces these assumptions only in a generic paragraph. Consider printing the scenario comment and the activity description with every prediction answer.
- **No evaluation yet.** A small benchmark of question templates with gold answers computed by SPARQL, plus a coverage figure for the help-panel questions, would let the paper report accuracy and abstention rates rather than assert them.
